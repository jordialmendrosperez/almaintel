The situation

Where they started.

Amazon sales, fees and refunds were keyed into QuickBooks by hand. Stock was bought in cases and sold in units, so inventory never quite matched, and the books rarely agreed with what Amazon actually paid out.

At month-end the accountant spent days trying to explain the difference.

What we built

The pieces.

01

Order pipeline

Each Amazon order becomes a sales receipt in QuickBooks, with its fees broken out.

02

Case-to-unit conversion

Purchases arrive in cases and are converted, so stock is always counted in units.

03

Refund and fee reconciliation

Reads Amazon’s event feed and matches every refund and fee to its order.

04

Daily exception check

Flags unmatched sales, refunds without an order and unmapped products. The target is zero.

A normal day

How it works now.

  1. Overnight, new orders, fees and refunds are pulled from Amazon.
  2. They are recorded in QuickBooks and matched to the payout.
  3. In the morning the team gets a short summary of anything that does not match.
  4. The accountant starts the month from books that already tie to Amazon.
