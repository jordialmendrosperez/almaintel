Where it gets stuck

The same receipt arrives through another channel. An extracted amount looks right, but its project is unclear. If the workflow writes directly to the system, speed at the first step can multiply the cost of correcting the last.

The intervention

The document is received with its source. Extraction proposes fields, checks look for inconsistencies and possible duplicates, and the responsible person confirms the action that changes the system.

The decision that matters

An assistant’s response is not the same as a recorded expense. The workflow needs to distinguish what it read, what it proposes and what was actually saved. The team needs to see those differences.

What we check

A hard-to-read photo, a repeated document, an unidentified project and a failed write. The workflow should only say a record is complete after the destination system confirms the write.

Scope and limits of the example

Document automation depends on source quality and available integrations. This is not an autonomous product ready for every business; scope needs to be defined and tested for each process.
